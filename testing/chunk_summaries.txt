===== CHUNK 8 =====
### QCOs (Quotas on Certain Products)

* Quotas are not as effective in powering exports.
* In the first year of a quota, India's exports increase by 10.6%.
* In the second quarter, exports decline by 12.8%.
* Over time, quotas do not lead to sustainable gains in exports.
* Quotas limit imports but do not boost exports growth.

-- Key Numbers --

* 10.6%: Increase in India's exports in the first year of a quota.
* 12.8%: Decline in India's exports in the second quarter.
* $131.5 million: India's toy exports in 2019.
* $167 million: India's toy exports in 2024.
* 5%: Annual growth rate of toy exports before quotas.
* Rs 10,000-15,000: Certification costs per consignment for small firms.
* 0.5 years: Maximum delay in certification approval for small businesses.

### Toy Industry

* Imports limited due to QCOs but exports did not see significant growth.
* Trade surplus achieved despite falling imports and stagnant exports.
* CAGR of less than 5% a year indicates slow growth.
* India's share of global footwear exports has fallen due to QCOs.

### Global Footwear Exports

* India's share decreased after implementing QCOs.
* Competitors like Vietnam saw an increase in textile exports.
* China plus one wave did not lead to significant export gains for India.

### Textile Industry

* Man-made fibre textile exports to major markets fell or stagnated after input QCOs.
* Domestic prices rose 15-30% above world levels for certain raw materials.
* Finished Indian products face a huge cost disadvantage in global markets due to increased costs.

### Steel Industry

* Restrictions on high-grade steel forced factories to operate below capacity.
* Domestic prices rose 15-30% above world levels for certain raw materials.

### Small and Medium-Sized Enterprises (SMEs)

* Burdened by QCOs, which raise entry barriers and costs.
* Certification costs alone can be as high as Rs 10,000-15,000 per consignment.
* Delays of over half a year can become too onerous for small businesses.

### Large Companies

* Have resources to meet strict standards set by QCOs.
* Can invest in testing labs and hire consultants to absorb delays.
* Still face challenges due to increased compliance costs.
