===== CHUNK 6 =====
### Summary
QCOs (Quality Control Orders) are a policy tool meant to achieve certain economic ends in India. They represent a philosophy that helps those who make raw materials at the cost of those who use them. QCOs amplify this dynamic across dozens of sectors, providing trade-offs between protecting domestic industry and making sectors less competitive.

### Key Numbers
- 2021: The year when the BIS set standards for toys covering various safety aspects.
- 7 to 8 months: The time required for procedures under QCOs, including paperwork and factory inspections.
- Dozens of sectors: Sectors that are affected by QCOs, amplifying the dynamic between protecting domestic industry and making sectors less competitive.

### Entities
- BIS (Bureau of Indian Standards): An organization responsible for setting standards for products in India.
- ISI (Indian Standard Institution): A mark that indicates compliance with Indian standards.


===== CHUNK 7 =====
### Summary

The Indian government has implemented Quality Control Orders (QCOs) to regulate the import of goods, particularly toys, and boost domestic manufacturing. The QCOs have been successful in reducing imports, with toy imports declining from $319 million in 2018 to $40 million in 2021. However, QCOs are not effective in promoting exports, with India's exports growth being limited. The Think Tank CSEP found that imports decline by 24% in the long term after a QCO is imposed, but QCOs do not lead to any sustainable gain in exports.

### Key Numbers

- $319 million: toy imports in 2018
- $40 million: toy imports in 2021
- 10 times: increase in usage of Quality Control Orders (QCOs) from 2018 to present
- 10.6%: growth in India's exports in the first year after a QCO is imposed
- 12.8%: decline in India's exports in the second quarter after a QCO is imposed
- $131.5 million: toy exports in 2019
- $167 million: toy exports in 2024
- <4%: CAGR of toy exports from 2019 to 2024
- 24%: decline in imports after a QCO is imposed, as found by the Think Tank CSEP

===== CHUNK 8 =====
### Summary
QCOs (Quotas and Certification Orders) have been found to be ineffective in powering exports from India, despite initial growth in the first year. The Centre for the Study of Economic Policy (CSEP) concludes that QCOs do not lead to sustainable gains in exports. Instead, they increase costs for manufacturers, particularly those producing raw and intermediate goods, leading to a bottleneck in industries with little domestic capacity. This results in higher prices for finished Indian products, making them less competitive in global markets.

### Key Numbers
- 10.6%: Increase in India's exports in the first year of a QCO.
- 12.8%: Decline in India's exports in the second quarter after implementing QCOs.
- $131.5 million: Initial value of India's toy exports in 2019.
- $167 million: Value of India's toy exports in 2024, with a CAGR of less than 5% per year.
- 5%: Annual growth rate of India's toy exports from 2019 to 2024.
- Rs 10,000-15,000: Certification costs for Indian companies under QCOs.
- 0.5 years: Maximum delay in certification approval for small businesses.

### Entities
- India
- Centre for the Study of Economic Policy (CSEP)
- China

### Summary
The discussion revolves around entry barriers and costs for small firms in India's quality control order (QCO) regime. Large companies can afford to meet strict standards due to their resources, but small businesses struggle. Certification costs and delays of over half a year become prohibitive. Recently, the government has reevaluated QCOs, recommending scrapping or suspending over 200 orders, mostly on intermediate inputs.
