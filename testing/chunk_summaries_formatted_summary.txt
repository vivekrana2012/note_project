===== CHUNK 6 =====
### Summary

* QCOs (Quality Control Orders) are a policy tool meant to achieve certain economic ends, representing a philosophy that the Indian government has operated with.
* They amplify trade-offs across dozens of sectors, favoring domestic industry over imports.
* QCOs require products to meet specific quality standards before manufacturing, importing, or selling in India.
* Compliance with QCOs can be time-consuming and costly, often favoring domestic producers.

### Key Numbers
- 2021: The year the BIS set many standards for toys covering various safety aspects.
- 7-8 months: The timeframe required to complete procedures for compliance with QCOs.
- Dozens of sectors: The number of industries affected by QCOs, which amplify trade-offs.

### Entities
- BIS (Bureau of Indian Standards)
- India


===== CHUNK 7 =====
### Summary
* The Indian government has implemented Quality Control Orders (QCOs) to regulate imports and promote domestic manufacturing.
* QCOs have been successful in reducing import dependence in certain sectors, such as the toy industry.
* However, their effectiveness in boosting exports is limited.

- $167 million: Indian exports of toys in 2024
- 24%: Decline in imports after a QCO is imposed (according to CSEP)
- 10.6%: Increase in India's exports in the first year of a QCO
- 12.8%: Decline in India's exports in the second quarter after a QCO

===== CHUNK 8 =====
### Summary

* The QCO (Quality Control Order) system does not lead to sustainable gains in exports for India.
* The industry saw limited export growth despite falling imports.
* QCOs have a negative impact on the toy industry, with exports growing at less than 5% per annum from 2019 to 2024.
* Indian textile exports fell or stagnated after input QCOs, while competitors like Vietnam saw their textile exports rise.
* India's share of global footwear exports has fallen due to QCOs.
* QCOs have increased costs for manufacturers and created bottlenecks in industries that rely on raw and intermediate goods.

### Key Numbers

- 10.6%: Increase in India's exports in the first year after implementing QCOs.
- 12.8%: Decline in India's exports in the second quarter after implementing QCOs.
- $131.5 million: Initial export value of the toy industry in 2019.
- $167 million: Export value of the toy industry in 2024, representing a CAGR of less than 5%.
- 5%: Annualized compound growth rate (CAGR) of the toy industry from 2019 to 2024.
- 15-30%: Increase in domestic prices of polyester fibres and specialty steels above world levels due to QCOs.
- Rs 10,000-15,000: Certification costs per consignment for Indian companies.

===== CHUNK 9 =====
### Summary

* Entry barriers and costs for small firms are a major concern due to high certification costs and delays, which can be as high as Rs 10,000-15,000 per consignment.
* The quality control order (QCO) system privileges large companies over small ones, leading to increased market concentration in QCO hit industries.
* A high-level government committee led by Niti Ayog was set up to look into this problem and recommended scrapping or suspending over 200 QCOs.
* The suspension of QCOs is expected to ease access to raw materials and reduce supply bottlenecks.

### Key Numbers

* Half a year: maximum delay for small businesses
* 200: number of QCOs recommended for scrapping or suspension by the Niti Ayog-led panel

* 28%: percentage of Reliance's exports that require fully compliant cargos with EU regulations
